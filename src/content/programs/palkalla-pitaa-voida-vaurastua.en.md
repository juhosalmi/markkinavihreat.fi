## Why is it so hard to build wealth on a salary?

Finland taxes work heavily. Progressive earned-income tax comes off your pay first, and you can only save and invest what is left after tax. On an ordinary salary, so little is left over that building wealth can feel impossible.

Investors already have ways to defer tax. Investment funds, insurance wrappers and the equity savings account let returns grow without every sale being taxed straight away. Good. We want the same principle to apply to wages: savings are taxed only when they are put to use.

That is exactly what a wealth account does. You can invest the euros you earn from your work before income tax is deducted, and your investments get an entirely new head start!

In practice, the wealth account is not a bank account but a bookkeeping arrangement in the Finnish Tax Administration's systems. Wages moved to the account are deductible in income taxation, and trades inside the account are not taxed. Tax is paid only when you withdraw money for your own use. The withdrawal is then split in two: the deposited wages are taxed as earned income, and investment returns as capital income, just as they are today.

This simple change promotes people's capitalism. When more and more wage earners are also owners, the economy becomes more resilient to crises and building wealth comes within reach of every wage earner.

Here are four reasons why the wealth account builds a better and fairer market economy:

> "Building wealth should not be the exclusive privilege of those who are already wealthy, but an opportunity open to everyone who works. We need a system that encourages ownership."

— The Market Greens

## Benefit 1/4: Invest your whole paycheck for a bigger starting capital

The real obstacle to building wealth on a salary is that tax has to be paid on your pay before a single euro can be invested. That puts workers behind those who already have capital.

With a wealth account, savings are deductible in income taxation. You can invest the full gross amount, and compound interest gets to work with a bigger engine from the very start.

The tax is not skipped; only its timing moves. Earned-income tax is paid when the deposit is withdrawn, and capital income tax is paid on the returns. Building capital is not penalised up front.

## Benefit 2/4: Invest freely wherever you want, the taxman doesn't set the terms

The Finnish tax system is full of exceptions that steer how people invest. The same investment is taxed differently depending on whether it sits in an equity savings account, in an insurance wrapper or in direct ownership.

That is inefficient for a market economy. In a wealth account, different investments stand on equal footing for tax purposes. Taxation no longer steers your decisions: you choose your investments by their return and risk, the way markets are meant to work.

## Benefit 3/4: Switching investments no longer locks up your money

Many Finns' savings are stuck in old investments they would like to get rid of. The capital gains tax due on selling stops them from switching.

The money sits in inefficient holdings instead of funding new Finnish innovations or growth companies. A wealth account creates no such lock-in. You can sell your investments and buy new ones without paying tax in between. Tax is paid only when you move the money from the account to your own use.

Capital flows to its most productive use, and our capital markets become more dynamic and agile.

## Benefit 4/4: Tax what you spend, not what you save

The idea behind the wealth account is simple: wages are taxed when they are spent, not when they are saved. Capital earned through work can grow undisturbed, and thrift and financial security are rewarded.

When the rules are the same for everyone, the need for costly tax planning shrinks. The system is also built so that deferred tax cannot be avoided altogether:

- **Moving abroad.** If you move abroad, the tax deferred on the account falls due in the same way as on a withdrawal. This is not a new tax but payment of tax already accrued before the funds leave Finland's taxing jurisdiction. It does not affect your other assets.
- **Death.** The account cannot be used to defer tax indefinitely. On death, the account is wound up and the deferred tax is paid in the estate's taxation in the same way as on a withdrawal.

This ensures that taxes get paid and the system stays fair to everyone.

## Let's make building wealth everyone's right

The wealth account tackles one of the biggest obstacles to Finns building wealth: how painfully hard it is to accumulate assets through diligent wage work. When savings can go into investments before tax is paid, we give work and enterprise the value they deserve.

This is the market economy at its best. Building wealth is not a privilege of those who already have capital; everyone gets the same rules for building their financial security. Tax-induced inefficiencies shrink, money flows to productive uses, and compound interest benefits all of Finland.

Join us in making people's capitalism real. Let's choose a solution that rewards hard work and brings a fair market economy within reach of every wage earner.
